VII. Hypothesis Testing:¶

  • H0: No significant relationships exist between variables
  • H1: Significant relationships exist between variables

1. Age vs MonthlySpend (Correlation Test)¶

•	H0: No correlation between Age and MonthlySpend
•	H1: Correlation exists
In [21]:
corr, p = stats.pearsonr(df['Age'], df['MonthlySpend'])

print(f"Correlation: {corr:.4f}")
print(f"P-value: {p:.4f}")

Correlation: -0.0123
P-value: 0.2030
* Correlation Test Results:¶
  • correlation: -0.012
  • p-value: 0.203
  • Decision: p-value > 0.05 → fail to reject H0
  • Conclusion: No significant relationship

2. Age vs Customer DaysSinceLastInteraction (Correlation Test)¶

•	H0: There is no correlation between Age and DaysSinceLastInteraction
•	H1: There is a correlation between Age and DaysSinceLastInteraction
In [22]:
corr, p = stats.pearsonr(df['Age'], df['DaysSinceLastInteraction'])

print(f"Correlation: {corr:.4f}")
print(f"P-value: {p:.4f}")

Correlation: -0.0040
P-value: 0.6817
* Correlation Test Result:¶
•	Correlation: -0.004
•	p-value: 0.682

Decision: • Since p-value > 0.05, we fail to reject the null hypothesis

Conclusion: • There is no significant relationship between age and customer activity. Older customers are not necessarily less active.

3. Gender vs Customer Activity (T-test)¶

•	H0: There is no difference in MonthlySpend between males and females
•	H1: There is a difference
In [23]:
male = df[df['Gender'] == 'Male']['DaysSinceLastInteraction']
female = df[df['Gender'] == 'Female']['DaysSinceLastInteraction']

stats.ttest_ind(male, female)

* T-test Results:¶
  • t-statistic: 1.67
  • p-value: 0.0949
  • Decision: p-value > 0.05 → fail to reject H0
  • Conclusion: No significant difference between groups

4. Education vs MonthlySpend (ANOVA)¶

•	H0: Mean MonthlySpend is same across all education levels
•	H1: At least one group is different
In [24]:
groups = [group['MonthlySpend'].values for name, group in df.groupby('Education')]

stats.f_oneway(*groups)
Out[24]:
F_onewayResult(statistic=np.float64(0.2288066867370917), pvalue=np.float64(0.922359467759936))
* ANOVA Test Result:¶
  • F-statistic: 0.23
  • p-value: 0.9223
  • Decision: p-value > 0.05 → fail to reject H0
  • Conclusion: Education level does NOT significantly affect MonthlySpend

VIII. Business Insights:¶

1.Customer spending is highly variable and right-skewed, indicating the presence of high-value customers who contribute disproportionately to revenue.

2. No significant relationship was found between age and spending, and education level does not significantly affect spending, suggesting that demographic-based targeting may not be effective.

3.Customer engagement (DaysSinceLastInteraction) does not significantly differ across genders, indicating uniform engagement behavior.

4.A strong relationship exists between marital status and number of pets, highlighting an opportunity for niche marketing (e.g., pet-related products).

5.Average spending across states appears relatively similar, indicating broadly consistent customer behavior across regions.

Additional Insights -¶

• Customers with higher monthly spending form a small but important segment contributing significantly to revenue.

• Since most variables show weak correlations, customer behavior is likely influenced by multiple factors rather than a single demographic variable.

• Customers with longer inactivity periods (high DaysSinceLastInteraction) represent potential churn risk and should be targeted with re-engagement campaigns.

• The presence of variability in spending suggests the opportunity to classify customers into low, medium, and high-value segments.

IX. Recommendations:¶

  • Personalize marketing based on spending and engagement behavior
  • Target high-spending customers with premium offers
  • Re-engage inactive customers through segmentation strategies
  • Use pet ownership and behavioural patterns for targeted campaigns
  • Monitor customer engagement regularly to reduce churn

# Executive summary:¶

This analysis used descriptive statistics, visualizations, and hypothesis testing to uncover patterns in customer behavior. Key insights include diverse spending behavior, minimal differences in engagement by gender or geography, and opportunities for pet-based and behavior-driven targeting. Recommendations include segmentation-based marketing campaigns to optimize revenue and re-engagement.

X. Conclusion:¶

Customer behavior shows high variability in spending. However, no strong statistical relationships were found between age, gender, or geography and key metrics.
